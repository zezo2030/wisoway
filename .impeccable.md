## Design Context

### Users
RedShare serves two user segments across Arab communities:
- **Everyday commuters** sharing rides between nearby cities on a regular basis
- **Occasional travelers** seeking affordable long-distance inter-city rides
- Users operate in both Arabic (primary, RTL) and English contexts
- Two distinct roles: **Drivers** (list trips, manage bookings, pay communication fees) and **Passengers** (browse, book, chat, rate)
- Context of use is mobile-first, often on-the-go — booking rides quickly while traveling or planning ahead

### Brand Personality
**Trusted. Simple. Local.**
- Voice is confident but approachable, never corporate or cold
- Arabic-first with full RTL support — the interface should feel native, not translated
- Community-oriented: connecting people through shared routes, not just transactions
- Modern without being trendy — built to last, not to chase design fads

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Clean & Minimal** — whitespace-heavy, restrained palette, let content breathe
- **Reference:** Uber / Careem — map-centric, professional, confident use of space
- **Anti-reference:** Never Western-only — designs must respect Arabic/RTL context, avoid generic English-first patterns
- **Primary brand color:** Teal (`#0D9488`) — deep, trustworthy, modern, and distinct from typical ride-hailing blue
- **Typography:** Tajawal (Arabic-optimized Google Font). Never mix with Cairo or other inline fonts. One font family, consistent weight hierarchy.
- **Icons:** Iconsax Plus (Linear for inactive, Bold for active states)
- **Light + Dark theme** support — design tokens must support both modes
- **Rounded, approachable shapes** — consistent border-radius scale (8, 12, 16, 20, 24)
- **Subtle shadows** — `black.withOpacity(0.04)`, blur 8-12, offset (0,2-4). Never heavy elevation.

### Design Principles

1. **Trust through clarity** — Every screen should communicate safety and reliability. Clear information hierarchy, no ambiguity in trip details, pricing, or driver/passenger info. Gender-based seating rules enforced visually.

2. **Arabic-first, not Arabic-after** — RTL is the default. Spacing, icons, and layouts should be designed for Arabic then adapted for English, not the reverse. Use Tajawal exclusively.

3. **One design system, zero drift** — No hardcoded colors, no inline font overrides, no one-off radius values. Every visual decision references a design token. The create-trip purple (#6C63FF) and Tailwind Slate palettes are design debt to eliminate.

4. **Speed of task** — Users are on-the-go. Minimize taps to core actions: book a seat, create a trip, check notifications. Remove decorative elements that slow comprehension.

5. **Teal as signal** — The teal brand color (#0D9488) is reserved for primary actions, active states, and brand moments. It should never be used for decorative or background fill — it draws the eye to what matters.
